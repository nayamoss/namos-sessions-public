# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Conference organizers are the primary users. They work under deadline pressure to collect
submissions, review and accept sessions, confirm speakers, gather required materials, resolve
schedule conflicts, and keep the program ready for publication. Reviewers and speakers use
narrower role-specific surfaces for assigned evaluations and their own portal work.

## Product Purpose

Takumi Talks is conference program-management software. It turns the post-CFP workflow into a
coherent, event-scoped operating system: submission review, speaker readiness, onboarding tasks,
communications, availability, and agenda scheduling. Success means users can see what needs
attention, take the next valid action, and trust that state persists without hidden inference.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, operational.

## Anti-references

- Do not copy Sessionboard's border-heavy, shadow-heavy visual treatment or its dull enterprise-blue surfaces; Takumi's electric blue is a focused interaction color, not a page wash.
- Do not turn operational pages into oversized metric dashboards or decorative card grids.
- Do not expose actions that are not connected to a real, authorized, persistent workflow.
- Do not trade information density for ornamental whitespace or novelty controls.

## Design Principles

1. Put the work before the report: the primary workflow belongs in the first viewport.
2. Make state honest: distinguish explicit user decisions from delivery events and derived hints.
3. Preserve context: let users inspect and update a record without losing their queue or filters.
4. Use one familiar component vocabulary across every organizer surface.
5. Design for deadline conditions: concise labels, dense scanning, resilient errors, and obvious
   recovery paths.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the baseline. All workflows must be keyboard operable with visible focus,
semantic names and status announcements, sufficient text and state contrast, non-color-only
meaning, zoom/text-scaling resilience, and reduced-motion support. Desktop and narrow mobile
layouts must preserve the same task completion path.
